Delper

A trust layer for global e-commerce

Executive summary: reaching 2 billion verified products and up to 3.5 billion protected buyers in five years.

Thesis: Delper is a buyer-first verified sourcing platform. Buyers post enquiries, vetted sellers bid in real time, independent QC checks the goods and escrow protects the money. Buyers pay no separate fee; sellers prepay QC and bidding at actual cost and pay a 2% commission on sale. Merchant platforms, payment processors and marketplaces extend our reach.

The problem

Online shoppers lose money to counterfeit goods, phantom stock and non-delivery. Buyers who source from factories face the same risk, with upfront payments and no independent check on quality. Existing fixes are fragmented: marketplace-owned assurance, separate inspection firms and generic escrow.

The solution

  • Customers are the kings. Buyers pay no separate fee for verification, QC, bidding or protection.
  • Demand source. Customers browse the static catalogues of sellers, which are free to list, and place an enquiry with Delper to avail independent 3PQC and the RTB. Those enquiries are the leads sellers pay for.
  • Tiered verification. Automated catalog and seller checks cover most items. Independent physical quality control (3PQC) is sampled and risk-targeted, because inspecting every product physically is impossible.
  • Real-time bidding (RTB). Buyers post product enquiries and only pre-vetted factories can bid live. Visibility is ranked by verified 3PQC pass rate and inventory data, not ad spend. Free to buyers; sellers pay for bidding in advance, at actual cost.
  • Third-party QC agent team (3PQC). Independent agents physically inspect high-risk and sampled orders and issue the sign-off code that clears the goods for dispatch. Agent pay is not tied to pass rates. Free to buyers; sellers pay for inspections in advance, at actual cost.
  • Conditional escrow. Buyer funds are held in escrow (Axis Bank partnership in progress) and released only after delivery and a satisfaction certificate from the customer.
  • Open verification record. A tamper-evident record, published as an open standard, that any merchant site, checkout or marketplace can display.

Equal opportunity for all sellers

  • Same terms for every seller, old or new. Ranking depends on verified 3PQC results and inventory data, not tenure, size or ad spend.
  • Quality products pass 3PQC. Sellers with quality products gain RTB visibility.
  • Low ratings cost nothing more. Products rated low by 3PQC incur no further QC or RTB expense for the seller.
  • Every product stays listed. All products remain listed for sale with their 3PQC rating shown, so the customer decides whether to buy or pass. If the customer buys a low-rated product, it ships with a caveat stating its 3PQC rating and that it cannot be rejected on the basis of that rating.

Draft caveat wording: "This product is rated [rating] by independent 3PQC. You chose to buy it knowing this rating, so it cannot be rejected on the basis of that rating. Your rights for non-delivery or damage are not affected."

3PQC delivery model

Proposed structure. SGS is the target lead partner; no agreement is in place yet.

  • Lead global provider: SGS, for physical inspection, testing and certification across major sourcing countries.
  • Regional alternates: one or two other accredited firms (for example Bureau Veritas or Intertek), rotated by region to avoid concentration.
  • Independent assurer: EY audits agent independence, pass-rate integrity and escrow controls across the network.

Safeguards: no pass-rate targets or fee linkage to outcomes in contracts; volume-based per-inspection rates tested in the Year 1 pilot; agreed service levels for capacity; conflict-of-interest checks against providers' existing clients.

Business process

Proposed roles. Partner agreements are not yet in place.

  • Delper: runs the RTB (seller vetting, rules, ranking and data) and the platform.
  • QC partner: SGS or an alternate does the inspections and issues the sign-off code, with no role in ranking.
  • Escrow bank: Axis Bank holds buyer funds and releases them after delivery and a customer satisfaction certificate (partnership in progress).
  • Independent assurer: EY audits the RTB, QC independence and escrow controls, with no design or operating role.
  • Operating partner (optional, growth stage): PwC runs business process under contract while Delper keeps the IP and ranking rules.
  • Sellers and buyers: take part but cannot change the ranking rules.

Year 1 pilot plan

A small, low-cost pilot to test the model before scaling. Targets are proposed.

  • Scope: India only, one product category, 6–9 months, with buyers from ONDC.
  • Participants: 30–50 vetted factories and 15–25 DTC brands ($5–50M revenue).
  • Volume: about 300–500 real orders, with manual bidding, human QC (SGS or another accredited firm; no agreement yet) and escrow with Axis Bank (in progress). Sellers prepay RTB and QC charges.
  • Budget: roughly $2.5–4M of the seed.
  • Measure: cost per order, defects caught, disputes and fraud avoided, repeat orders, and whether a processor or marketplace will pay for protection.
  • Gate to Series A: continue only if cost per order shows a path to covering itself and several brands reorder.

Business model

  • Customers are the kings: buyers pay no separate fee for QC, RTB or protection.
  • Sellers pay RTB and QC charges in advance, at actual cost. These are a non-refundable seller expense, separate from the 2% commission.
  • Sellers pay a 2% sales commission when a sale completes, deducted at escrow release.
  • Sellers who agree to take part in the RTB also agree to the lead generation cost being deducted from their sale proceeds only upon escrow release, which supports repeat sales. It is earned through 19 types of events.
  • Event costs are borne by sellers.
  • Taxes and FX conversion costs are charged to the buyer extra at checkout.
  • The USD 300 average buyer price covers lead generation cost, product cost, sales commission, doorstep delivery, and QC and RTB cost.
  • Sellers can list a static catalogue free, with no onboarding fee. To conduct business, sellers pay a USD 100 annual business onboarding fee, plus RTB and QC charges at actual cost, to be certified as a Healthy Seller and take part in the RTB.
  • Buyer funds are held in escrow; the Axis Bank partnership is in progress.

Why it can work

  • Partners (merchant platforms, payment processors, marketplaces) extend reach, while buyer-first terms attract demand.
  • Sellers pay commission only when they sell, and verified status gives honest sellers visibility and buyer trust.
  • Reach scales by riding existing cards, accounts and platforms.

Viability and next steps

What works

  • Real problem: fraud, non-delivery and quality disputes cost buyers and sellers money.
  • Coherent core: independent QC, quality-ranked bidding and escrow released after delivery fit together.
  • Small first bet: an India-only pilot funded by a $4M seed, released in tranches on preferred terms.

Open issues

  • Nothing is signed: Axis Bank, SGS and ONDC are not yet agreed, and EY and PwC are only proposed.
  • No team or legal entity shown: investors back the founding team first.
  • Unit economics are unproven: QC at about $9 per order is an assumption, and a physical inspection of a USD 300 order may cost more, so QC may need to be sampled at batch level.
  • Seller burden: charges paid to or through Delper are about 27% of the price, and sellers prepay QC and RTB non-refundably and wait up to 15 days after delivery to be paid.
  • ONDC fit: Year 5 volume of 7.5M orders is about 7% of ONDC annual transactions in mid-2026, which are mostly mobility and food.
  • Forecast drivers are assumptions: sellers, orders per seller, QC, delivery and product costs must be replaced with pilot data.

Next steps

  • Months 0 to 2: name the founding team, set up the legal entity, and get signed letters of intent from Axis Bank, SGS (or an alternate) and ONDC participants.
  • Months 2 to 9: run the paid India pilot, starting with about 50 orders, and measure cost per order, seller acceptance of the charges, QC pass-rate integrity and repeat purchases.
  • Months 9 to 12: replace the assumptions with pilot data, reset the seed and Series A pricing on traction, and raise the Series A only if the agreed targets are met.

Assessment: viable as a narrow India pilot, and investable once the open issues above are closed. This rests on assumptions made in this document and is not investment advice.
